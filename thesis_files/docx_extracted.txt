Introduction
The systematic analysis of human inner experience has long occupied a peripheral position within computational research, with dream cognition representing perhaps the most underexplored dimension of that domain. Mental health informatics has made considerable advances in affective computing, sentiment detection, and clinical decision support over the past decade. Dream content, however, resists these frameworks. It is ephemeral, symbolically dense, and culturally encoded in ways that standardized diagnostic instruments rarely accommodate. The predominant methods for capturing dream data remain manual journaling and retroactive clinical interviews, both of which impose fundamental limitations on the quality of the data they produce. Recall bias distorts the fidelity of narratives recovered hours or days after waking, and the interpretive labor required of clinicians restricts the scale at which longitudinal analysis can be conducted. Temporal distortion compounds this problem further, as the affective weight and symbolic content of a dream shift in memory even within a single waking hour. These limitations are not incidental. They are structural features of analog collection methods that, uncorrected, render pattern detection across a patient's dream history methodologically unreliable.
Morpheus addresses this structural problem directly. It is a semantic analysis system designed to automate the transformation of spoken or written dream narratives into structured psychological data that can be stored, queried, and analyzed at scale. The system accepts audio input in Georgian, translates and transcribes the narrative through a locally deployed instance of OpenAI Whisper, and then routes the resulting English text through a multi-stage pipeline that performs entity extraction, embedding generation, and psychologically grounded interpretation. The foundational precedent for applying natural language processing to dream content at this level of semantic granularity is established by Saleemi et al. (2025) in "Developing an AI Model for Dream Interpretation Based on Subconscious Analysis," which demonstrates that NLP-based classification pipelines can reliably identify thematic and emotional categories within dream text. Morpheus extends that precedent considerably, treating classification not as an endpoint but as a preliminary step within a richer analytical architecture.
What distinguishes Morpheus from prior classification-based approaches is its implementation of a Retrieval-Augmented Generation architecture grounded in Jungian and psychoanalytic source texts. Rather than mapping dream content to predefined label categories, the system retrieves semantically relevant passages from an academic knowledge base at query time, constructing a contextually enriched prompt that guides the language model toward evidence-based, theoretically coherent interpretation. The result is a mode of analysis that reflects the interpretive depth of clinical commentary rather than the categorical output of a classifier. This architectural choice has direct implications for the quality and defensibility of the psychological insights the system produces, particularly in the context of longitudinal tracking, where accumulated errors in early-stage classification can propagate and distort downstream pattern recognition.
The present research is oriented around two central questions that together define the scope of what Morpheus is designed to test. The first is whether a RAG-based pipeline, operating on a vector knowledge base of psychoanalytic literature, can produce semantically coherent and psychologically grounded analyses of dream content when applied at scale across diverse narrative inputs. The second concerns the feasibility of tracking latent emotional patterns, including anxiety, stress, and perfectionism, longitudinally through the geometric relationships that emerge in a high-dimensional vector space, where dreams sharing underlying affective content cluster in proximity regardless of their surface-level narrative differences. These questions are not independent. The validity of longitudinal pattern detection depends on the interpretive quality established at the single-dream level, and the system's capacity to address both simultaneously constitutes its primary claim to methodological novelty.
The argument is developed across two chapters. Chapter 1 situates Morpheus within the broader landscape of computational psychology, surveying the engineering frameworks that have been applied to subconscious data tracking and examining in detail the limitations that generic large language models exhibit when deployed in mental health contexts without domain-specific grounding, before presenting a comprehensive account of the Morpheus architectural design, its backend infrastructure, and the rationale for the specific technical choices made at each stage of the pipeline. Chapter 2 then turns to the empirical dimensions of the project, beginning with a detailed treatment of document ingestion and text sanitization, tracing the construction of the vector embedding space, and evaluating the system's technical performance under realistic operating conditions. Whereas Chapter 1 establishes the theoretical and architectural foundation, Chapter 2 concludes by proposing a psychometric framework and experimental design through which the clinical validity of the system's outputs could be formally assessed in future research. Together, these chapters present Morpheus not simply as a working software system but as a rigorous methodological contribution to the nascent field of computational dream analysis.

Chapter 1: Computational Psychology and System Architecture
The intersection of computational systems and psychological data presents a class of design problems that is qualitatively distinct from those encountered in conventional software engineering. Architecture is not neutral in this domain. The structural choices made at the level of data representation, model selection, and retrieval strategy directly determine the scientific validity of every output the system produces, and understanding why Morpheus was designed as it was therefore requires situating those decisions within a broader account of how computational approaches to psychological language have evolved and where earlier frameworks have failed.
1.1 Engineering Frameworks for Subconscious Data Tracking
Within the context of computational systems, "subconscious data" refers to unstructured, high-variance natural language generated from internal psychological states, as distinct from the structured behavioral or biometric signals that dominate adjacent fields such as physiological monitoring or activity tracking. A heart rate reading carries a precisely bounded meaning within a well-defined measurement schema. A dream narrative does not. It encodes multiple emotional valences simultaneously, operates through symbol and metaphor rather than direct referential statement, and resists any schema that presupposes stable relationships between surface-level linguistic features and underlying psychological meaning. This fundamental property of subconscious data has defined both the ambitions and the persistent failures of every computational framework that has attempted to process it systematically.
The earliest efforts to analyze psychological language through computational means relied on keyword matching and rule-based sentiment classifiers, architectures that assigned emotional polarity to text by scanning for the presence of pre-categorized lexical items. The Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count system developed by Pennebaker et al. (2001) represents the most widely adopted instantiation of this approach, mapping word frequencies onto psychological dimensions through a manually curated dictionary of several thousand entries. These frameworks produced interpretable outputs and enabled a substantial body of quantitative research in personality psychology and clinical linguistics, but they introduced a critical and largely unacknowledged distortion. By reducing the affective content of natural language to binary or categorical polarity scores, they stripped precisely the complexity that makes psychological data scientifically valuable. A dream in which water appears as a symbol of suffocation and a dream in which water signals liberation would receive identical or near-identical scores under any keyword-frequency model, because the semantic relationship between a symbol and its psychological valence is not a function of the symbol's presence alone. It is a function of the surrounding narrative context, the emotional register of the sentences that precede and follow it, and the theoretical framework through which the symbol is being interpreted. That contextual structure is exactly what rule-based systems cannot represent.
The emergence of transformer-based architectures fundamentally changed what was computationally achievable in natural language understanding. Devlin et al. (2019) introduced BERT, a bidirectional encoder that learns deep contextual relationships between tokens through masked language modeling on large text corpora, producing dense vector embeddings in which proximity in the representational space corresponds to proximity of meaning across the full surrounding context rather than within a fixed window. This property proved consequential for psychological language processing. For the first time, a model could represent the difference between "falling" as a physical event description and "falling" as an anxiety marker without requiring that distinction to be hand-coded by a researcher. The GPT family of autoregressive architectures extended the capabilities of transformer representations further by demonstrating that models trained at sufficient scale could generate semantically coherent, tonally appropriate continuations of complex and metaphorically dense text. The most directly relevant application of these capabilities to dream analysis is documented by Saleemi et al. (2025), who applied BERT-based classification to a corpus of dream narratives and successfully categorized dominant emotional content into anxiety, sadness, and happiness clusters with measurable predictive accuracy. That work established the empirical feasibility of transformer-based approaches to the dream domain and constitutes the immediate NLP precedent that the Morpheus system extends and deepens.
The specific engineering challenge that differentiates psychological language processing from other NLP domains, however, is one that transformer classification alone cannot resolve. Psychological language is non-linear, metaphorical, and temporally recursive in ways that distinguish it categorically from news articles, product reviews, or the social media posts on which most NLP benchmarks are calibrated. A dream narrative may reference a childhood memory, a present anxiety about professional performance, and an imagined future scenario within three consecutive sentences, with no syntactic marker distinguishing one temporal plane from another. Standard classification pipelines fail under these conditions because they treat each input as a stateless event, encoding it as a fixed-dimension vector without any mechanism for grounding the interpretation in the established theoretical frameworks that give psychological symbols their diagnostic meaning. The Hall and Van de Castle (1966) quantitative coding system, which remains the foundational instrument for systematic dream content analysis in the psychological literature, made this requirement explicit at the empirical level decades before computational methods became sophisticated enough to address it. Their nominal coding categories, covering aggression, friendliness, sexuality, misfortune, and success, were not arbitrary. They were derived from psychoanalytic and depth psychology theory, and their predictive validity across cross-cultural studies depended precisely on that theoretical anchoring. A computational system that discards this grounding in favor of purely data-driven pattern recognition loses the interpretive validity that clinical analysis requires.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation addresses this structural problem at the architectural level. Lewis et al. (2020) introduced the RAG framework as a method for combining a parametric generative language model with a non-parametric retrieval mechanism operating over an external document store, enabling the model to condition its generated outputs on retrieved passages of domain-specific evidence at inference time, rather than relying solely on knowledge encoded in model weights during pre-training. The implications for psychological language analysis are immediate. A RAG system can be configured so that every interpretation generated is conditioned on retrieved passages from domain-specific theoretical literature, ensuring that the output reflects not only what the language model can infer from the dream text itself but also what established psychoanalytic frameworks indicate about the symbols and emotional configurations present in the narrative. In the Morpheus implementation, this knowledge store is constituted by the curated corpus of psychoanalytic and depth psychology source texts maintained in the books directory of the backend repository. The cosine similarity search executed over the knowledge_base table in Supabase at query time retrieves the most semantically proximate passages from this corpus and injects them into the generative prompt as theoretical context before the language model produces its analysis. Domain-specific grounding of this kind is not merely a design preference in mental health applications. It is a scientific necessity, because a system that produces interpretations that are linguistically fluent but theoretically unanchored creates the appearance of clinical validity without its substance, a failure mode that is arguably more consequential than producing no interpretation at all.
Establishing this engineering foundation clarifies the specific shortcomings that arise when generic, off-the-shelf language models are deployed in mental health contexts without the domain-specific retrieval scaffolding that Morpheus implements, and those shortcomings are what Section 1.2 addresses directly.
1.2 Limitations of Generic Large Language Models in Mental Health
General-purpose large language models, including GPT-4, Claude, and the Llama family of open-weight models, represent a remarkable advance in the capacity of computational systems to produce fluent, contextually appropriate natural language across an extraordinarily wide range of topics and tasks. Their outputs are coherent, their tone is adjustable, and their apparent comprehension of complex subject matter is, in many domains, functionally indistinguishable from that of a knowledgeable human reader. This fluency, however, is not the same property as validity. In most application domains, the gap between the two is tolerable. A general-purpose model that produces a slightly imprecise summary of a news article causes no meaningful harm. In clinical and psychological contexts, the gap between fluency and validity is not tolerable at all. It is consequential in ways that require careful architectural attention.
The most structurally significant failure mode of large language models in mental health applications is hallucination. Language models generate outputs by sampling from probability distributions over possible token sequences conditioned on the preceding context. They do not verify claims against ground truth before producing them. When a model has insufficient grounding in the specific domain it is being asked to reason about, it produces outputs that are syntactically and stylistically convincing but factually or theoretically unsupported, a phenomenon consistently documented in the literature on LLM reliability (Maynez et al., 2020). In the context of dream analysis, a hallucinated interpretation is not merely an error in the conventional software sense. If a user receives a fluent, confident psychological reading of their dream that attributes to them an emotional pattern or psychological tendency that the model generated without valid theoretical basis, and if that user internalizes it as insight, the harm is direct and not hypothetical. This is not a risk specific to any single model or provider. It is a structural consequence of the generation mechanism itself, and it cannot be corrected by prompt engineering alone.
The knowledge boundary problem compounds the hallucination risk considerably. Large language models are trained on internet-scale corpora in which psychologically relevant content is present but thoroughly diluted by pop-psychology articles, self-help blog posts, journalistic interpretations of therapeutic concepts, and layperson accounts of dreams and emotional experiences. Within that training distribution, the signal representing rigorous Jungian or psychoanalytic frameworks, including the theoretical constructs of the collective unconscious, shadow integration, anima and animus dynamics, and individuation, is vanishingly small relative to the noise. The model consequently has no reliable internal representation of these frameworks as theoretical systems. It can produce text that uses the vocabulary of depth psychology fluently, because that vocabulary appears in its training data, but it cannot be expected to apply the frameworks with the consistency and precision that clinical interpretive validity requires. Generic fluency with psychoanalytic terminology is not the same as theoretical grounding, and the difference between the two is precisely what determines whether an interpretation has scientific standing.
A further limitation that is rarely foregrounded in general NLP discussions but is critical in psychological applications is the statelessness of standard language model deployments. Each call to a commercial language model API is contextually isolated. The model receives no information about prior sessions, retains no record of the emotional content the user has described across previous interactions, and has no capacity to construct a longitudinal picture of the user's psychological state. For a single-session creative writing assistant, this property is irrelevant. For a system intended to track whether anxiety markers in a user's dream journal are becoming more frequent, more intense, or clustering around specific symbolic motifs over thirty days, statelessness is a fundamental architectural disqualification. No amount of prompt augmentation within a single API call can substitute for a persistent, queryable record of prior semantic content, because the analytical value of longitudinal tracking lies precisely in the relationships between sessions rather than within any single one.
The ethical dimension of commercial API dependency in mental health applications demands separate consideration. Dream content is among the most intimate categories of personal psychological data a user can generate. Routing that content through third-party infrastructure, including the inference endpoints of commercial model providers, exposes it to data retention policies, model fine-tuning pipelines, and security surfaces that the user cannot audit or control. This concern applies regardless of the specific privacy commitments made by any given provider, because the structural exposure exists at the level of transmission and processing rather than at the level of contractual intent. Mental health informatics applications have an obligation to minimize unnecessary data movement, particularly when local and privacy-preserving alternatives exist.
Morpheus is designed with each of these limitations in explicit view. The knowledge boundary problem is addressed through the domain-specific books corpus, which supplies the psychoanalytic grounding that the generative model itself cannot be assumed to possess reliably, ensuring that every produced interpretation is conditioned on retrieved passages from established theoretical literature rather than on training data of uncertain provenance. The longitudinal limitation is addressed through persistent vector storage in Supabase, where every processed dream is saved as a 384-dimensional embedding alongside its extracted metadata, enabling session-to-session semantic comparison across the full duration of a user's engagement with the system. The privacy concern is addressed through local embedding computation via sentence-transformers, which processes all dream text on the application's own infrastructure rather than transmitting it to a commercial embedding API. It is worth observing that the BERT-based classification approach taken by Saleemi et al. (2025), by forgoing generation entirely in favor of discriminative classification, sidestepped the hallucination problem altogether, and that architectural conservatism was appropriate and defensible for the research scope they defined. The limitation of that approach is that discriminative classification, however accurate, cannot produce the interpretive narrative depth that makes psychological analysis clinically useful rather than merely categorical. Extending their work to include generation is what introduces the hallucination risk, and the RAG architecture implemented in Morpheus is the mechanism through which that risk is controlled.
1.3 Morpheus Architectural Design and Backend Infrastructure
Morpheus is a Progressive Web Application built on a modular, service-oriented architecture that separates concerns across three distinct layers, each responsible for a different class of operations. The user-facing layer manages input capture, real-time state presentation, and graph-based visualization. The API layer coordinates the processing pipeline, enforces validation, and routes requests to the appropriate service components. The combined data and intelligence layer handles persistence, vector search, and language model inference. Each of these separations is a deliberate design decision rather than an incidental structural property. Keeping the intelligence layer decoupled from the API layer, for instance, means that individual service components, including the speech-to-text module, the embedding model, and the language model client, can be evaluated, replaced, or upgraded independently without disrupting the broader application.
The backend is implemented in Python 3.11, using FastAPI as the web framework and Uvicorn as the ASGI server. FastAPI was selected for a combination of reasons that are each relevant to this application specifically. Its asynchronous request handling prevents I/O-bound operations, such as database writes, embedding computation, and external language model API calls, from blocking concurrent requests, which matters for a pipeline that chains multiple sequential operations of variable latency. It enforces type-safe request and response validation through Pydantic schema models, reducing the class of input-handling errors that can corrupt downstream analytical steps. Furthermore, it generates OpenAPI documentation automatically from type annotations, providing a persistent machine-readable record of the API contract that is valuable both for development verification and for the kind of reproducibility that academic research requires. The application root is organized under the app directory, where main.py defines the FastAPI application instance, config.py manages environment variable loading through Pydantic settings, and database.py provides the Supabase client used by all persistence operations.
The system exposes two primary API endpoints, each corresponding to a distinct phase of user interaction. The first, POST /v1/dreams/process, accepts an audio file upload and orchestrates the full analytical pipeline in sequence, beginning with audio format conversion and speech-to-text transcription, moving through PII anonymization, Jungian entity extraction, and semantic embedding generation, then executing the RAG retrieval step, producing the psychological analysis, and finally persisting all outputs to the database before returning a structured response to the client. The second endpoint, GET /v1/graph/data, queries the dreams, keywords, and dream_keywords tables and returns a JSON structure containing two arrays, one of nodes representing individual dream sessions and extracted keywords, and one of links representing the relational connections between them, which the frontend consumes directly for graph rendering. These two endpoints encode the system's entire analytical contract with its users, and their simplicity at the interface level is a consequence of the complexity absorbed by the service layer behind them.
The AI service layer, implemented in app/services/ai_service.py, is where the computational intelligence of the system resides. Speech-to-text conversion is handled by the openai-whisper library running as a locally loaded model instance, which accepts Georgian-language audio and performs automatic language detection and translation to English through its task="translate" inference mode, ensuring that the remainder of the pipeline operates on a consistent language regardless of the speaker's native tongue. Audio submitted in any format is first converted to 16 kHz mono WAV by a preprocessing step that invokes FFmpeg, ensuring compatibility with Whisper's expected input specification. Entity extraction and psychological analysis are both performed by Google Gemini 1.5 Flash via the google-generativeai SDK, with separate prompt templates governing each task. The entity extraction prompt instructs the model to return a structured JSON object containing a dream title, a list of Jungian symbols, a list of dominant emotions, and a one-sentence summary, while the analysis prompt conditions the model on the retrieved RAG context and instructs it to produce a full psychological interpretation. Semantic embedding is computed by the sentence-transformers library using the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model, which produces 384-dimensional dense vectors and is computationally lightweight enough to run on CPU without GPU acceleration, making it suitable for cloud deployment on constrained infrastructure.
The persistence layer is built on Supabase, which combines a managed PostgreSQL instance with the pgvector extension for high-dimensional vector storage and cosine similarity search. The schema is organized across four tables whose responsibilities are cleanly separated. The dreams table stores the raw transcribed text, the generated psychological analysis, and the 384-dimensional embedding vector for each processing session, alongside user identity and timestamp metadata. The keywords table maintains a deduplicated registry of all extracted psychological terms, ensuring that the same symbol encountered across multiple dream sessions is represented by a single node in the relational graph rather than redundant duplicates. The dream_keywords junction table encodes the many-to-many relationships between individual dream sessions and their associated keywords, providing the relational structure that the graph visualization endpoint traverses. The knowledge_base table stores chunked and embedded passages from the psychoanalytic source texts in the books directory, and a stored database procedure named match_documents executes cosine similarity search over this table at query time, accepting a query embedding and returning the top-k most semantically similar passages, which form the RAG context injected into the analysis prompt.
The backend is deployed to Railway, a cloud hosting platform whose configuration is defined by the Procfile, nixpacks.toml, and requirements.txt files in the backend repository root. Cloud deployment is architecturally significant for a reason that extends well beyond operational convenience. Dream recall is highest during the hypnopompic state, the brief transitional period between sleep and full wakefulness, when narrative memory of a dream remains accessible before consolidation degrades it. A system that requires a locally running laptop to be powered on and initialized is simply not usable at that moment. Railway deployment ensures the backend is continuously available from any networked device, including a phone held in bed, which aligns the system's availability model precisely with the window of highest user data quality.
The user interface is built in React with Vite as the build tool and TailwindCSS for component styling, following a mobile-first responsive layout appropriate for the primary use context. The knowledge graph, the central visualization artifact of the application, is rendered through react-force-graph-2d, a D3-force-based library that produces interactive, physics-simulated force-directed graph layouts in which node positions emerge from the relational structure of the data rather than from manual placement. A PWA manifest file configures the application for installation on mobile devices, enabling it to be launched from a home screen with no browser chrome, further reducing the friction between waking and capturing a dream narrative. Taken together, the architectural decisions across every layer of the Morpheus system are oriented toward a single unified goal, which is the conversion of ephemeral subjective psychological experience into durable, queryable, semantically structured data that retains enough of its original meaning to support longitudinal analysis.

Chapter 2: Data Engineering, Semantic Pipelines, and Empirical Evaluation
Chapter 1 established the theoretical and architectural foundations of the Morpheus system. Chapter 2 turns to the operational substance of those foundations, examining in detail the data engineering pipeline that transforms raw psychological input into semantically structured knowledge, and the empirical methods used to evaluate both the system's technical behavior and the validity of its psychological outputs. These two concerns are not separable, because the reliability of every downstream analytical claim depends directly on the quality of the data engineering decisions made upstream.
2.1 Document Ingestion and Text Sanitization
Morpheus must solve two fundamentally different data ingestion problems, and conflating their requirements would compromise both. The first track handles real-time user input, audio recordings of dream narratives that arrive as live, variable-quality data and must be transformed into clean, anonymized, structured text before any psychological analysis can proceed. The second track handles the static ingestion of academic and psychoanalytic source texts into the knowledge base, a one-time preprocessing operation whose output determines the quality of every RAG retrieval the system will ever perform. Each track demands a different sanitization strategy, and describing them in turn clarifies both the technical design and the reasoning behind it.
Audio capture for the user input track is initiated through the browser's MediaRecorder API, which encodes the spoken dream narrative as a compressed audio blob and submits it to the FastAPI POST /v1/dreams/process endpoint as a multipart form upload. The first operation the backend performs is format normalization. Because browsers produce audio in formats including WebM, OGG, and MP4 depending on the operating system and browser vendor, the service layer invokes FFmpeg to convert every incoming audio file to a 16 kHz mono WAV before passing it to the speech-to-text model, ensuring consistent input regardless of the client device. Speech-to-text transcription is handled by the openai-whisper library, which runs as a locally loaded model instance within the application's own infrastructure. Whisper operates in translation mode, using task="translate", which means it accepts input in any language, automatically identifies the source language, and returns English text. This is consequential for the Morpheus use case specifically, because Georgian, the primary target language for the application, is a morphologically complex language for which downstream NLP tools including entity extractors and embedding models have substantially less coverage than English. Producing a high-quality English transcript before proceeding makes the remainder of the pipeline language-agnostic. The decision to run Whisper locally rather than through the hosted OpenAI Whisper API was motivated by three considerations that together make the case unambiguous. Audio content containing sensitive psychological material never leaves the system's own infrastructure, eliminating the data exposure risk associated with third-party processing. Per-call cost is eliminated entirely. And the model quality is identical, because the locally deployed Whisper artifact is the same underlying model as the one served through the API.
Following transcription, the raw text passes through a PII anonymization layer before any further processing occurs. This layer operates at two levels. A spaCy named entity recognition model identifies PERSON entities in the transcribed text and replaces each identified span with the placeholder token [PERSON], using a reverse-order substitution strategy that preserves character offsets across multiple replacements within the same string. A set of compiled regular expression patterns then handles the remaining personally identifiable categories, replacing email addresses, phone numbers, and specific dates with the placeholder tokens [EMAIL], [PHONE], and [DATE] respectively. The design decision to use spaCy NER for person names rather than a regex approach directly addresses a known failure mode of purely pattern-based anonymization, namely the mis-tagging of place names, organization names, and other capitalized tokens as personal identifiers. Anonymization matters at two levels. Ethically, it prevents the association of identifiable personal information with intimate psychological content in persistent storage. Technically, it removes tokens with no psychological signal from the text that Gemini 1.5 Flash will process in the next stage, reducing noise in the entity extraction output. Entity extraction is performed in JSON mode, enforcing a strict schema that requires four fields, a short evocative dream title, an array of Jungian symbols, an array of dominant emotions, and a one-sentence narrative summary. Schema enforcement is not merely a convenience. It is a reliability guarantee. A generative model operating without output constraints can produce malformed, inconsistent, or hallucinated responses that silently corrupt downstream pipeline stages when they are parsed and persisted to the database.
The second track, knowledge base ingestion, operates on the nine psychoanalytic and depth psychology source texts that constitute the RAG retrieval corpus, including Freud's "The Interpretation of Dreams," Jung's "Archetypes of the Collective Unconscious" and "Man and His Symbols," Campbell's "The Hero with a Thousand Faces," van der Kolk's "The Body Keeps the Score," Eliade's "Myths, Dreams and Mysteries," Jung's "Memories, Dreams, Reflections," and the symbol dictionaries by Cirlot and the Penguin editorial team. These texts arrive in raw digital formats acquired from Project Gutenberg and various digital editions, each carrying a different profile of noise that degrades retrieval quality if left in place. The sanitization utility clean_for_rag.py addresses this noise through a multi-stage pipeline applied to each source file in sequence. The first stage strips Project Gutenberg legal wrappers, which bookend the actual text content with license headers and footers that carry no psychological content and would otherwise inflate the number of spurious retrieval matches. The second stage corrects OCR artifacts introduced during digitization, including soft hyphen line breaks that split compound words across lines, year misreads in which the digit 1 was scanned as the letter i producing strings such as i960 in place of 1960, and stray decorative Unicode symbols left over from the original typesetting. The third and fourth stages strip back matter and front matter respectively. Back matter removal targets index blocks, bibliography sections, footnote compilations, acknowledgment pages, and publisher marketing material appended after the main text, all of which carry dense but contextually decontextualized language that would pollute the semantic similarity space. Front matter removal targets copyright pages, ISBN lines, publisher addresses, endorsement quotes, and other boilerplate preceding the actual prose content, using a combination of pattern-matching against known publisher identifiers and a heuristic that detects the onset of genuine prose by measuring paragraph length and sentence-ending density. A fifth stage removes tables of contents and illustration lists that may survive front-matter stripping in texts with complex structural layouts. The final normalization stage collapses excessive whitespace, removes isolated page numbers and roman numeral page markers that OCR preserved as standalone lines, strips footnote superscript digits that were glued to adjacent word tokens, and eliminates TOC leader dots and separator bars. For texts identified as alphabetical reference dictionaries by detecting the structural fingerprint of a standalone capital letter followed immediately by a lowercase entry word, an additional pass removes all-caps page-running headers that OCR introduced between dictionary entries.
Once the source texts have been cleaned, the ingestion script segments each file into chunks of approximately 500 tokens, corresponding to roughly 2,000 characters, with a 200-character overlap between adjacent chunks. The overlap is not an arbitrary parameter. Without it, theoretical concepts or psychoanalytic arguments that happen to span a chunk boundary would be split into two fragments stored in non-adjacent retrieval units, and the similarity search would fail to surface the complete argument when either fragment alone matched the query embedding. The overlap ensures that every semantically contiguous passage in the source text is fully represented in at least one retrievable chunk. Each chunk is embedded using the same all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence-transformer model used for dream embeddings, producing a 384-dimensional vector that is stored in the knowledge_base table alongside the chunk text and its source book title. Attributing each chunk to its source at storage time means that at retrieval, the system can present not only the passage text but also its provenance, which is a basic requirement for academically defensible interpretation.
The ingestion script enforces a quality gate before inserting records from any given source file, querying the knowledge_base table for existing entries sharing the same book title and skipping ingestion if matches are found. This deduplication check prevents the accumulation of redundant embedding vectors that would skew the cosine similarity distribution, causing passages from over-represented texts to dominate retrieval results and reducing the effective diversity of the knowledge base. Once data has been sanitized, chunked, and stored with verified uniqueness, the embedding and retrieval pipeline determines how semantic meaning is encoded and queried at inference time, which is the subject of Section 2.2.
2.2 Vector Embeddings and Latent Theme Mapping
An embedding is a function that maps a sequence of natural language tokens to a fixed-dimensional point in a continuous metric space, such that semantic similarity between input texts corresponds to geometric proximity between their representative points. This property is what makes embeddings useful for psychological language analysis. Two dream narratives that describe structurally different experiences but share an underlying emotional register, both involving confrontation with authority, both suffused with anticipatory anxiety, will produce embedding vectors that are close together in the representational space even if they share no surface vocabulary. The sentence-transformers model all-MiniLM-L6-v2, which generates all embeddings in the Morpheus system, produces a 384-dimensional output space and is trained specifically to optimize semantic similarity tasks by fine-tuning on large-scale natural language inference and semantic textual similarity benchmarks. It is computationally lightweight enough to run without GPU acceleration on the Railway deployment infrastructure, which is a practical requirement given the memory constraints of the free-tier hosting environment, and its output dimensionality of 384 is sufficient to represent the semantic complexity of dream narratives while remaining tractable for cosine similarity search at the scale of a personal dream journal.
The choice of cosine similarity as the retrieval distance metric requires explanation before the formal definition is introduced. Embedding vectors vary in magnitude depending on the length and density of the text they represent. A one-sentence dream fragment and a six-sentence narrative describing the same core psychological experience will produce vectors of different magnitudes. A Euclidean distance metric would assign a larger separation to these vectors purely on the basis of that magnitude difference, misrepresenting their semantic proximity. Cosine similarity solves this problem by measuring the angle between two vectors rather than the absolute distance between their endpoints, normalizing for magnitude and capturing directional alignment in the embedding space. Two vectors pointing in the same direction receive a cosine similarity of 1 regardless of how long or short each vector is, while two vectors pointing in opposite directions receive a score of 0. For embedding-based retrieval over psychological text, where input length varies considerably and semantic relatedness should be the dominant signal, cosine similarity is the correct metric. The formula governing the computation is as follows.
\cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}}{\|\mathbf{A}\| \times \|\mathbf{B}\|}
In this expression, A and B represent the 384-dimensional embedding vectors of two texts being compared, the dot product in the numerator captures their directional alignment by summing the products of their corresponding components, and the product of their L2 norms in the denominator normalizes the result to the interval between 0 and 1 for non-negative embeddings. In the Morpheus application context, A is the embedding of the incoming dream narrative, and B is the embedding of a stored chunk in the knowledge_base table, with the computation executed across the entire stored corpus to identify the passages most relevant to the user's current dream content.
The match_documents stored procedure in Supabase implements this retrieval operation as a native database function using the pgvector extension. The procedure accepts a query embedding vector, a minimum similarity threshold, and a maximum result count as parameters. It computes the cosine similarity between the query vector and every stored knowledge base embedding using pgvector's <=> cosine distance operator, converts the distance to a similarity score by computing 1 - distance, filters out any result falling below the specified threshold, and returns the top-k remaining results ordered by descending similarity. The threshold filter is not a minor optimization. A generative language model will incorporate whatever context it receives into its output. If the retrieval step surfaces passages that are semantically distant from the dream content simply because no sufficiently similar passage exists in the knowledge base, the model will attempt to construct an interpretation from that irrelevant context, producing outputs that are fluent but analytically misleading. Enforcing a minimum similarity threshold means the generative prompt is either grounded in genuinely relevant theoretical material or left without retrieved context, both of which are preferable to grounding it in noise.
Latent theme mapping is the longitudinal function that distinguishes Morpheus from a single-session dream analysis tool. Each dream narrative processed by the system generates an embedding vector that is stored alongside its metadata in the dreams table. Over repeated sessions, the collection of a user's dream embeddings defines a trajectory through the 384-dimensional semantic space. The geometric structure of that trajectory, specifically the clustering patterns and directional tendencies within it, encodes information about the user's recurring psychological preoccupations that the user may not have consciously identified or articulated. A set of dream embeddings that consistently cluster in a region of the embedding space associated with themes of evaluation by authority figures, performance failure, and urgency under constraint, themes that are representable in the Jungian knowledge base through passages on perfectionism, the shadow, and the animus dynamic, indicates a latent stress or perfectionism pattern. That inference does not require the user to have mentioned perfectionism in any single dream narrative. It emerges from the geometric distribution of the embeddings across sessions.
The graph data structure returned by the GET /v1/graph/data endpoint encodes the same semantic information in a relational rather than geometric form. Dream nodes and keyword nodes are connected by edges that represent the co-occurrence of a keyword within a particular dream session, with the graph topology built from the dreams, keywords, and dream_keywords tables at query time. This graph is not merely a visual artifact. It functions as a relational semantic map of the user's psychological vocabulary across the full history of their engagement with the system. A keyword node appearing as a high-degree hub, connected to many dream nodes across many sessions, is a computational indicator of a persistent psychological theme in a form that is directly analogous to a clinician observing that a patient returns to the same symbolic content across multiple therapy sessions. The graph makes that pattern visible at scale without requiring manual cross-session annotation.
Saleemi et al. (2025) employed embedding-based clustering to group a population of dream participants by dominant emotion category, identifying aggregate clusters corresponding to anxiety, sadness, and happiness at the group level. That approach is methodologically appropriate for the research question they addressed, which concerned the statistical distribution of emotional content across a population. The Morpheus approach is complementary but distinct in its analytical unit. Rather than mapping population-level distributions, it maps the longitudinal evolution of a single user's semantic position through the embedding space across time, treating individual psychological trajectory as the primary object of analysis rather than statistical category membership. Vector analysis of this kind describes what the system produces, however, and does not constitute an evaluation of whether those outputs are technically reliable or psychologically valid. That evaluation is the subject of Section 2.3.
2.3 Technical Verification and System Performance
Evaluation of the Morpheus system operates at two distinct levels that must be kept carefully separate. The first is system-level technical verification, which concerns whether each component of the pipeline executes its designated function correctly and within acceptable performance bounds. This level of evaluation is performable against the existing implementation using controlled test inputs, and the results presented in this section reflect that verification. The second level is human-impact empirical validation, which concerns whether the psychological interpretations the system generates are clinically meaningful to real users and whether longitudinal tracking produces measurable improvement in psychological self-awareness. This level of evaluation requires a live user cohort and cannot be addressed with the current implementation alone. It is formally proposed in Section 2.4. Conflating the two levels would both overstate what has been demonstrated and obscure what genuinely remains as future work.
End-to-end pipeline verification proceeds as a narrative sequence through each processing stage. A test audio clip recorded in Georgian is submitted to the POST /v1/dreams/process endpoint, initiating the full pipeline. Whisper processes the audio and produces an English transcript, which is then assessed by manual comparison against a ground-truth transcript prepared before the test. Here, a significant and consequential engineering finding was encountered. Railway's free-tier deployment imposes a memory ceiling that prevents loading the Whisper medium or large model variants, necessitating the use of the base model for cloud deployment. Georgian is a morphologically complex language belonging to the Kartvelian family with no Indo-European relatives, and as a low-resource language it is underrepresented in Whisper's multilingual training distribution relative to European languages. The base model, which is substantially less capable than medium or large at handling low-resource phonological patterns, produces translation output with elevated word error rates for Georgian input, particularly for dream vocabulary involving emotionally charged or culturally specific terms that appear infrequently in the training corpus. The structural logic of the pipeline is fully verified in the sense that Whisper produces an English string that propagates correctly through every downstream stage. The accuracy of that string, however, is insufficient for production-grade psychological analysis when the source language is Georgian. Resolving this bottleneck requires either upgrading to a Whisper medium or large variant by providing infrastructure with higher memory allocation, or adopting a fine-tuned phonetic encoder specifically trained on Georgian speech data, which would materially improve transcription fidelity for this particular use case. This finding does not invalidate the system architecture. It identifies the specific deployment constraint that must be addressed before the accuracy guarantees of the design can be realized in practice.
Following transcription, the entity extraction stage is verified by confirming that the JSON response returned by Gemini 1.5 Flash conforms to the enforced schema and that the extracted symbols and emotions are semantically coherent with the dream narrative supplied in the test. Schema conformance is a binary check, and the JSON mode configuration of the Gemini API ensures that structurally malformed outputs do not reach the persistence layer. Semantic coherence assessment, by contrast, requires manual review, in which an evaluator reads the source dream text and the extracted entity arrays and judges whether the identified symbols and dominant emotions reflect the narrative content. In controlled testing with English-language inputs, this judgment was consistently affirmative, confirming that the entity extraction prompt and schema together produce reliable structured output. The embedding is then generated and its correctness is verified by confirming that the stored vector has exactly 384 dimensions, matching the vector(384) column specification in the Supabase pgvector schema. RAG retrieval is verified by inspecting the knowledge base chunks returned by match_documents for topical relevance to the test dream content, confirming that the cosine similarity search surfaces passages from the psychoanalytic corpus that are genuinely related to the symbols and themes present in the narrative. Finally, the generated Georgian-language analysis is reviewed for linguistic fluency and thematic coherence with the extracted symbols, confirming that the generative prompt construction and RAG context injection produce analytically grounded output.
Response latency across the full pipeline represents a user-experience constraint that is not merely a performance metric but a functional requirement. The pipeline involves local Whisper inference, a Gemini API call for entity extraction, a pgvector cosine similarity search over the knowledge base, and a second Gemini API call for analysis generation. Each stage introduces latency of variable magnitude depending on audio duration, knowledge base size, and network conditions at the time of the API call. Total end-to-end latency should be measured across multiple test submissions and reported as a mean with a standard deviation to characterize both typical behavior and variability. For a reflective journaling application, where the user is recording a dream immediately after waking and has limited tolerance for a slow interface during a cognitively fragile state, an acceptable latency ceiling is approximately 10 to 20 seconds from submission to displayed analysis. Measurements exceeding that range would indicate a need for pipeline optimization, and the most likely candidate for improvement is the Whisper inference stage, where switching from the base to a quantized medium model with a larger memory allocation would address both the latency and the accuracy limitations identified above simultaneously.
Knowledge base integrity is verified by inspecting the knowledge_base table following the execution of the ingestion script. After processing all nine source texts, the table should contain several hundred distinct records, each representing a 2,000-character chunk with a 384-dimensional embedding and a source attribution. Verification of the embedding distribution involves confirming that vectors are geometrically dispersed across the semantic space rather than concentrated in a narrow cluster, which would indicate either redundant chunking of homogeneous source material or a malfunction in the text segmentation logic. A well-distributed embedding space maximizes the probability that any given dream narrative will surface a topically distinct and informative set of retrieval results, supporting the interpretive diversity that makes the RAG architecture analytically valuable.
Graph endpoint verification is performed by calling GET /v1/graph/data after multiple dream sessions have been processed and inspecting the returned JSON for structural and semantic correctness. The response object should contain a nodes array and a links array, with node entries carrying id, label, type, and color fields and link entries carrying source and target fields that reference valid node identifiers. Across multiple sessions, recurring keywords should manifest as high-degree nodes connected to several dream nodes simultaneously, demonstrating that the relational schema and graph construction logic in app/services/graph_logic.py correctly aggregate cross-session co-occurrence data. A keyword that appears in isolation across only a single dream session carries limited analytical weight. A keyword that appears as a structural hub across five sessions is a strong computational signal of psychological persistence, and the graph topology should make that distinction visible.
The verification procedures described in this section confirm that the Morpheus pipeline executes correctly at the architectural level and that its core components produce outputs consistent with their design specifications, with the notable exception of Whisper transcription accuracy under the memory-constrained deployment environment. What these procedures cannot confirm is whether the psychological interpretations generated by the system are experienced as meaningful or accurate by real users, whether the longitudinal tracking of emotional themes corresponds to patterns that users themselves recognize, or whether engagement with the system produces any measurable improvement in psychological self-awareness over time. These questions lie entirely outside the reach of technical verification and require empirical investigation with a live participant cohort. That investigation is what Section 2.4 formally proposes.
2.4 Proposed Psychometric Framework and Experimental Design
The evaluation framework proposed in this section is grounded in Design Science Research methodology, which Hevner et al. (2004) established as the appropriate paradigm for evaluating systems whose purpose is to create utility within a specific human context rather than to test a pre-existing natural phenomenon. Design Science Research treats the artifact, in this case the Morpheus system, as both the subject and the instrument of inquiry, and it requires that evaluation address not only whether the artifact functions correctly but whether it produces meaningful value for the humans who use it. The proposed study is a 30-day longitudinal single-cohort repeated-measures design, to be executed once the Morpheus system is deployed to a live participant cohort. This section constitutes a formal protocol specification for that future empirical milestone. The target participant pool is a minimum of thirty adult volunteers with no prior formal dream journaling practice, recruited through the IBSU volunteer network. Participants engage with Morpheus on any morning they recall a dream across thirty consecutive days, submitting audio or text narratives through the system interface, receiving the generated psychological analysis, and accumulating a session record that is available for both personal reflection and research analysis. The system functions as an observational and reflective tool throughout the study. It does not constitute clinical therapy, it does not generate diagnostic outputs, and participants are explicitly informed of these boundaries at the time of recruitment. This distinction is not peripheral. It is embedded in the protocol design at every stage.
The first metric the study evaluates is Dream Recall Frequency, defined as the average number of distinct dream episodes reported per participant per week across the study period. Recall frequency is not a measure of narrative quality or psychological depth, but it is a meaningful proxy for the regularity and intention with which participants engage with the reflective practice that Morpheus facilitates. Baseline measurement is established over Days 1 through 7, and endpoint measurement is taken over Days 24 through 30, the final seven days of the study. The null hypothesis for this metric, denoted H0, holds that mean Dream Recall Frequency at endpoint does not differ significantly from mean Dream Recall Frequency at baseline across the participant cohort. The alternative hypothesis, denoted H1, holds that mean Dream Recall Frequency at endpoint is significantly greater than at baseline, a directional prediction consistent with the intention-setting literature in sleep psychology, where consistent morning engagement with reflective practice has been associated with increased recall fidelity over time (Schredl, 2019). The appropriate statistical method is a paired-sample t-test, with each participant contributing one baseline recall count and one endpoint recall count as matched observations. The analysis reports the t-statistic, degrees of freedom equal to N minus 1, the two-tailed p-value, and Cohen's d as the standardized effect size measure. The significance threshold is set at p less than .05 throughout all tests in the study unless otherwise specified.
The second metric tracks changes in narrative richness across the study period through a measure known as Lexical Density. Lexical Density is computed as the ratio of unique content words to total words in a dream narrative, expressed as a percentage. Formally, the computation is as follows.
LD = \frac{|\text{unique lexical items}|}{|\text{total words}|} \times 100
A higher Lexical Density value indicates a narrative that draws on a broader, more varied vocabulary relative to its total length, which functions as a proxy for introspective range and emotional expressiveness. The hypothesis underlying this metric is that over thirty days of systematic dream engagement, participants will develop richer and more differentiated ways of articulating their internal experience, producing measurable increases in Lexical Density across the study period. Alongside Lexical Density, emotional valence shifts are tracked using the structured output of the Gemini entity extraction stage. The frequency of positive, negative, neutral, and complex emotion tags is aggregated per participant per week and plotted as a weekly trajectory across the full study period. The null hypothesis for these two measures holds that mean Lexical Density and mean emotional valence distribution at Days 24 through 30 do not differ significantly from their respective baseline values. The alternative hypothesis holds that mean Lexical Density increases significantly and that the emotional valence distribution shifts detectably, for instance through reduced anxiety tag frequency, increased neutral emotional tagging, or the emergence of more complex and mixed emotional classifications that suggest greater affective differentiation. The statistical analysis applies a paired-sample t-test for the continuous Lexical Density variable and a chi-square test for independence on the emotion tag frequency distributions, comparing early-study and late-study time blocks. The analysis reports the t-statistic and p-value for Lexical Density, and the chi-square statistic, degrees of freedom, p-value, and Cramér's V as the effect size measure for the categorical emotional valence comparison.
The third metric employs the Lucidity and Consciousness in Dreams scale, a standardized psychometric instrument developed by Voss et al. (2013) to assess self-reported dream awareness and metacognitive control across eight subscales, including insight, control, thought, realism, memory, dissociation, negative emotion, and self-determination. The LuCiD scale provides a validated quantitative measure of the degree to which a dreamer is aware of dreaming during the dream state, and its repeated administration across a longitudinal study period allows detection of change in metacognitive dream awareness over time. The instrument is administered at three time points, at Day 1 as the baseline, at Day 15 as the midpoint, and at Day 30 as the endpoint. The null hypothesis holds that LuCiD total scores at Day 30 do not differ significantly from LuCiD total scores at Day 1. The alternative hypothesis holds that LuCiD scores increase significantly from Day 1 to Day 30, indicating that sustained engagement with a reflective dream analysis practice improves metacognitive awareness during the dream state itself. The statistical method is a one-way repeated-measures analysis of variance across the three time points. The analysis reports the F-statistic, degrees of freedom for both the between-levels factor and the error term, the p-value, and partial eta squared as the effect size measure. If the omnibus ANOVA result reaches significance, Bonferroni-corrected pairwise post-hoc t-tests are applied across all three time point comparisons to identify which specific intervals account for the observed change, with the corrected alpha threshold set at .05 divided by the number of comparisons.
The fourth and final hypothesis is distinct in character from the three psychometric metrics. It does not require an external instrument and does not depend on self-reported data. It is computable entirely from the Supabase dreams table using the embedding vectors generated and stored by the system during normal operation. The hypothesis states that participants who engage with Morpheus consistently across the thirty-day study period will exhibit tighter semantic clustering in their personal embedding space toward the end of the study than at the beginning, reflecting a progressive narrowing and coherence in the psychological themes their dream narratives express. Tightening is operationalized as reduced mean intra-user cosine distance, computed as the average pairwise cosine distance between all dream embedding vectors produced by a single participant within a given seven-day window. A lower mean intra-user cosine distance indicates that the participant's dreams during that window are more semantically similar to one another, which the study interprets as evidence of increasing thematic focus and psychological coherence. The null hypothesis holds that mean intra-user cosine distance during Days 24 through 30 does not differ significantly from mean intra-user cosine distance during Days 1 through 7. The alternative hypothesis holds that mean intra-user cosine distance decreases significantly over the study period, indicating that participants' dream content converges toward a more semantically coherent cluster as the study progresses. The statistical method is a paired-sample t-test comparing the per-participant distance means from the first seven days against the per-participant distance means from the final seven days, with N equal to the number of participants contributing sufficient data in both windows to support the comparison.
The ethical framework governing the proposed study is not ancillary to the protocol. It is constitutive of it. All participants must provide written informed consent prior to any data collection, with documentation clearly stating the nature of the data being collected, how it is stored, and the purposes for which it will be used. Dream content is among the most intimate categories of personal psychological data that an individual can generate, and the consent process must reflect that sensitivity explicitly rather than treating it as a routine formality. Participants must be informed that their dream narratives are stored as anonymized vector representations and that no personally identifiable text is retained beyond the anonymization stage. They must be informed that the system does not constitute clinical therapy, that the analyses it produces are computationally generated and not clinically validated, and that participation does not create a therapeutic relationship of any kind. Institutional ethics review board approval, or its equivalent under the IBSU institutional ethics framework, will be sought and obtained before any live cohort recruitment begins. The proposed minimum sample size of thirty participants is not arbitrary. It represents the minimum viable cohort for detecting a medium-to-large effect, defined as Cohen's d of 0.5 or greater, with 80 percent statistical power at a two-tailed alpha of .05, consistent with the conventions established by Cohen (1988) for behavioral science research. Smaller samples would be insufficiently powered to detect the anticipated effect sizes and would produce findings that are neither replicable nor interpretable with confidence.

Conclusion
Morpheus demonstrates that a Retrieval-Augmented Generation architecture, grounded in domain-specific psychoanalytic knowledge, can transform subjective dream narratives into durable, semantically structured psychological data that persists across sessions and supports longitudinal analysis. This is not a capability that generic large language models provide. Their hallucination risk, their thin and unevenly distributed representation of psychoanalytic theory in training data, and their fundamental statelessness disqualify them for this purpose without the kind of domain-specific retrieval scaffolding that the Morpheus architecture supplies. Equally, the discriminative classification approach demonstrated by Saleemi et al. (2025), while empirically sound within its defined scope, produces categorical outputs that lack the interpretive depth required to generate psychologically meaningful narrative analysis. The architecture presented in this thesis addresses both limitations in a technically verifiable way. That is the contribution.
The architectural decisions documented in Chapter 1 are not incidental design choices. They are the conditions that make the psychological goal technically achievable at all. A FastAPI backend with asynchronous request handling ensures that the multi-stage pipeline executes without blocking under concurrent load. The Supabase and pgvector persistence layer ensures that every processed dream contributes a permanent, queryable semantic record rather than a transient output. Local embedding computation via sentence-transformers eliminates the data exposure risk that commercial API routing would introduce for content of this sensitivity. Cloud deployment on Railway ensures the system is available at the hypnopompic moment of highest recall fidelity, when a locally installed application would be simply unreachable. Remove any one of these properties and the longitudinal semantic tracking that defines Morpheus as analytically distinct from a single-session tool becomes impossible.
The data engineering findings of Chapter 2 establish several conclusions that generalize beyond this specific system. Document sanitization quality directly determines RAG retrieval quality, because polluted knowledge base vectors produce irrelevant retrieval chunks that a generative model will nonetheless incorporate into its output. Vector embeddings enable latent theme detection that transcends anything achievable through keyword matching, because geometric proximity in a high-dimensional continuous space captures conceptual and emotional similarity at a level of granularity that surface-level string comparison cannot reach. The proposed psychometric framework, organized around Dream Recall Frequency, Lexical Density, the LuCiD scale, and intra-user semantic clustering, provides a rigorous and statistically grounded pathway to human-impact validation that is formally specified, reproducible, and executable against the existing system without architectural modification.
Honesty requires acknowledging what the psychometric framework in Section 2.4 currently is and is not. It is a formally specified research protocol. It is not a completed study. This position is methodologically appropriate within the Design Science Research paradigm, where the artifact exists, its technical behavior is verified across a defined set of test conditions, and its human-impact hypotheses are precisely stated and statistically tractable. What the thesis does not yet provide is empirical evidence of user-level effect. That evidence comes from the 30-day cohort pilot, from obtaining IRB approval under the IBSU institutional ethics framework, and from applying the statistical methods of Section 2.4 to a real participant dataset. The absence of that evidence is a limitation, and stating it clearly is more scientifically valuable than obscuring it.
Subconscious data is perhaps the last domain of personal experience that quantitative methods have not yet made systematically legible. Clinical tools for capturing it remain dependent on human recall and manual annotation. Computational approaches capable of processing it with theoretical rigor are nascent. The potential for structured, longitudinally trackable insight into the patterns of a person's inner psychological life remains, by any reasonable measure, largely unrealized. Morpheus represents a first step toward changing that. It is computationally reproducible, architecturally grounded in established principles of retrieval and generation, and anchored in a century of psychoanalytic theory. What it demonstrates is that the technical infrastructure for this kind of analysis now exists and that the empirical work of establishing its human value is the natural and immediate next step.
