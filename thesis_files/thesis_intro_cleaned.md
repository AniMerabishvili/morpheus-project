## Introduction

Computational research has long treated the systematic analysis of human inner experience as a peripheral concern. Dream cognition may be the least explored dimension of that domain. Over the past decade, mental health informatics has advanced considerably in affective computing, sentiment detection, and clinical decision support. Dream content resists these frameworks nonetheless. It is ephemeral, symbolically dense, and culturally encoded in ways that standardized diagnostic instruments rarely accommodate.

Manual journaling and retroactive clinical interviews remain the predominant capture methods. Both impose fundamental limitations on data quality. Recall bias distorts narratives recovered hours or days after waking. Clinicians must perform interpretive labor that restricts the scale at which longitudinal analysis can proceed. Temporal distortion compounds the problem: within a single waking hour, the affective weight and symbolic content of a dream can shift in memory. These are not incidental limitations. They are structural features of analog collection that, left uncorrected, render cross-session pattern detection methodologically unreliable.

Morpheus addresses this structural problem directly. The system automates the transformation of spoken or written dream narratives into structured psychological data—data that can be stored, queried, and analyzed at scale. It accepts Georgian audio input, translates and transcribes each narrative through a locally deployed instance of OpenAI Whisper, and routes the resulting English text through a multi-stage pipeline. That pipeline performs entity extraction, embedding generation, and psychologically grounded interpretation. In "Developing an AI Model for Dream Interpretation Based on Subconscious Analysis," Saleemi et al. (2025) establish the foundational precedent for applying natural language processing to dream content at this level of semantic granularity, demonstrating that NLP-based classification pipelines can reliably identify thematic and emotional categories within dream text. Morpheus extends that precedent by treating classification not as an endpoint but as a preliminary step within a richer analytical architecture.

Prior classification-based approaches map dream content to predefined label categories. Morpheus takes a different path. It implements a Retrieval-Augmented Generation architecture grounded in Jungian and psychoanalytic source texts. At query time, the system retrieves semantically relevant passages from an academic knowledge base and constructs a contextually enriched prompt. That prompt guides the language model toward evidence-based, theoretically coherent interpretation. The output reflects the interpretive depth of clinical commentary rather than the categorical output of a classifier. This choice directly affects the quality and defensibility of the psychological insights the system produces—especially in longitudinal tracking, where accumulated classification errors can propagate and distort downstream pattern recognition.

Two central research questions define what Morpheus is designed to test. First: can a RAG-based pipeline, operating on a vector knowledge base of psychoanalytic literature, produce semantically coherent and psychologically grounded analyses of dream content at scale across diverse narrative inputs? Second: can latent emotional patterns—including anxiety, stress, and perfectionism—be tracked longitudinally through the geometric relationships that emerge in a high-dimensional vector space? In that space, dreams sharing underlying affective content cluster in proximity regardless of surface-level narrative differences. The questions are coupled. Longitudinal pattern detection depends on interpretive quality at the single-dream level; addressing both simultaneously is the system's primary claim to methodological novelty.

The thesis develops this argument across two chapters. Chapter 1 situates Morpheus within computational psychology. It surveys engineering frameworks for subconscious data tracking, examines the limitations generic large language models exhibit in mental health contexts without domain-specific grounding, and presents the Morpheus architectural design, backend infrastructure, and rationale for each pipeline-stage technical choice. Chapter 2 examines the empirical dimensions. It details document ingestion and text sanitization, traces construction of the vector embedding space, and evaluates technical performance under realistic operating conditions. Where Chapter 1 establishes theoretical and architectural foundations, Chapter 2 proposes a psychometric framework and experimental design through which future research could formally assess clinical validity of the system's outputs. Together, the chapters present Morpheus not as working software alone but as a rigorous methodological contribution to the nascent field of computational dream analysis.

## Chapter 1: Computational Psychology and System Architecture

Computational systems applied to psychological data pose design problems qualitatively distinct from those encountered in conventional software engineering. Architecture is not neutral here. Choices at the level of data representation, model selection, and retrieval strategy directly determine the scientific validity of every output the system produces. Explaining why Morpheus was designed as it was therefore requires situating those decisions within a broader account of how computational approaches to psychological language have evolved—and where earlier frameworks have failed.

### 1.1 Engineering Frameworks for Subconscious Data Tracking

Within computational systems, "subconscious data" denotes unstructured, high-variance natural language generated from internal psychological states. This stands in contrast to the structured behavioral or biometric signals that dominate adjacent fields such as physiological monitoring or activity tracking. A heart rate reading carries a precisely bounded meaning within a well-defined measurement schema. A dream narrative does not. It encodes multiple emotional valences simultaneously. It operates through symbol and metaphor rather than direct referential statement. It also resists any schema that presupposes stable relationships between surface-level linguistic features and underlying psychological meaning. This property has defined both the ambitions and the persistent failures of every computational framework that has attempted to process subconscious data systematically.

The earliest efforts relied on keyword matching and rule-based sentiment classifiers—architectures that assigned emotional polarity by scanning for pre-categorized lexical items. Pennebaker et al. (2001) developed the most widely adopted instantiation of this approach in the Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count system, mapping word frequencies onto psychological dimensions through a manually curated dictionary of several thousand entries. These frameworks produced interpretable outputs and enabled substantial quantitative research in personality psychology and clinical linguistics. They also introduced a critical and largely unacknowledged distortion. By reducing affective content to binary or categorical polarity scores, they stripped precisely the complexity that makes psychological data scientifically valuable. A dream in which water appears as a symbol of suffocation and a dream in which water signals liberation would receive identical or near-identical scores under any keyword-frequency model. The semantic relationship between a symbol and its psychological valence is not a function of the symbol's presence alone. It depends on the surrounding narrative context, the emotional register of adjacent sentences, and the theoretical framework through which the symbol is interpreted. Rule-based systems cannot represent that contextual structure.

Transformers changed what was computationally achievable in natural language understanding. Devlin et al. (2019) introduced BERT, a bidirectional encoder that learns deep contextual relationships between tokens through masked language modeling on large text corpora. BERT produces dense vector embeddings in which proximity in representational space corresponds to proximity of meaning across full surrounding context rather than within a fixed window. This property proved consequential for psychological language processing. For the first time, a model could distinguish "falling" as a physical event from "falling" as an anxiety marker without hand-coded rules. The GPT family extended these capabilities further, demonstrating that models trained at sufficient scale could generate semantically coherent, tonally appropriate continuations of complex and metaphorically dense text.

Saleemi et al. (2025) document the most directly relevant application of these capabilities to dream analysis. They applied BERT-based classification to a corpus of dream narratives and successfully categorized dominant emotional content into anxiety, sadness, and happiness clusters with measurable predictive accuracy. That work established the empirical feasibility of transformer-based approaches to the dream domain and constitutes the immediate NLP precedent that Morpheus extends and deepens.

Psychological language processing nevertheless presents an engineering challenge that transformer classification alone cannot resolve. Psychological language is non-linear, metaphorical, and temporally recursive—it differs categorically from news articles, product reviews, or the social media posts on which most NLP benchmarks are calibrated. A dream narrative may reference a childhood memory, a present anxiety about professional performance, and an imagined future scenario within three consecutive sentences, with no syntactic marker distinguishing one temporal plane from another. Standard classification pipelines fail here because they treat each input as a stateless event. They encode it as a fixed-dimension vector without grounding interpretation in the theoretical frameworks that give psychological symbols their diagnostic meaning.

Hall and Van de Castle (1966) made this requirement explicit at the empirical level decades before computational methods could address it. Their quantitative coding system remains the foundational instrument for systematic dream content analysis in the psychological literature. Their nominal coding categories—aggression, friendliness, sexuality, misfortune, and success—were not arbitrary. They derived them from psychoanalytic and depth psychology theory, and cross-cultural predictive validity depended precisely on that anchoring. A computational system that discards this grounding in favor of purely data-driven pattern recognition loses the interpretive validity that clinical analysis requires.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation addresses this problem at the architectural level. Lewis et al. (2020) introduced RAG as a method for combining a parametric generative language model with a non-parametric retrieval mechanism operating over an external document store. The model conditions its generated outputs on retrieved passages of domain-specific evidence at inference time rather than relying solely on knowledge encoded in model weights during pre-training. For psychological language analysis, the implications are immediate. A RAG system can configure every interpretation to draw on retrieved passages from domain-specific theoretical literature. The output then reflects not only what the language model infers from the dream text itself but also what established psychoanalytic frameworks indicate about the symbols and emotional configurations present in the narrative.

In Morpheus, the knowledge store consists of a curated corpus of psychoanalytic and depth psychology source texts maintained in the `books` directory of the backend repository. At query time, cosine similarity search over the `knowledge_base` table in Supabase retrieves the most semantically proximate passages from this corpus and injects them into the generative prompt as theoretical context before the language model produces its analysis. Domain-specific grounding of this kind is not merely a design preference in mental health applications. It is a scientific necessity. A system that produces linguistically fluent but theoretically unanchored interpretations creates the appearance of clinical validity without its substance—a failure mode arguably more consequential than producing no interpretation at all.

With this engineering foundation in place, Section 1.2 examines the shortcomings that arise when generic, off-the-shelf language models are deployed in mental health contexts without the domain-specific retrieval scaffolding Morpheus implements.

### 1.2 Limitations of Generic Large Language Models in Mental Health

General-purpose large language models—including GPT-4, Claude, and the Llama family of open-weight models—represent a remarkable advance in the capacity of computational systems to produce fluent, contextually appropriate natural language across an extraordinarily wide range of topics and tasks. Their outputs are coherent. Their tone is adjustable. In many domains, their apparent comprehension of complex subject matter is functionally indistinguishable from that of a knowledgeable human reader. Fluency, however, is not the same property as validity. In most application domains, the gap between the two is tolerable. A general-purpose model that produces a slightly imprecise summary of a news article causes no meaningful harm. In clinical and psychological contexts, the gap is not tolerable at all. It is consequential in ways that require careful architectural attention.

Hallucination is the most structurally significant failure mode of large language models in mental health applications. Language models generate outputs by sampling from probability distributions over possible token sequences conditioned on the preceding context. They do not verify claims against ground truth before producing them. When a model lacks sufficient grounding in the specific domain it is asked to reason about, it produces outputs that are syntactically and stylistically convincing but factually or theoretically unsupported—a phenomenon consistently documented in the literature on LLM reliability (Maynez et al., 2020). In dream analysis, a hallucinated interpretation is not merely a conventional software error. If a user receives a fluent, confident psychological reading that attributes an emotional pattern or psychological tendency the model generated without valid theoretical basis, and if that user internalizes it as insight, the harm is direct and not hypothetical. No single model or provider owns this risk. It follows from the generation mechanism itself, and prompt engineering alone cannot correct it.

The knowledge boundary problem compounds the hallucination risk considerably. Large language models train on internet-scale corpora in which psychologically relevant content is present but thoroughly diluted by pop-psychology articles, self-help blog posts, journalistic interpretations of therapeutic concepts, and layperson accounts of dreams and emotional experiences. Within that training distribution, the signal representing rigorous Jungian or psychoanalytic frameworks—including the collective unconscious, shadow integration, anima and animus dynamics, and individuation—is vanishingly small relative to the noise. The model consequently lacks a reliable internal representation of these frameworks as theoretical systems. It can produce text that uses depth psychology vocabulary fluently, because that vocabulary appears in its training data. It cannot be expected to apply the frameworks with the consistency and precision that clinical interpretive validity requires. Generic fluency with psychoanalytic terminology is not the same as theoretical grounding, and the difference between the two determines whether an interpretation has scientific standing.

Statelessness presents a further limitation rarely foregrounded in general NLP discussions but critical in psychological applications. Each call to a commercial language model API is contextually isolated. The model receives no information about prior sessions. It retains no record of emotional content described across previous interactions. It cannot construct a longitudinal picture of the user's psychological state. For a single-session creative writing assistant, this property is irrelevant. For a system intended to track whether anxiety markers in a user's dream journal are becoming more frequent, more intense, or clustering around specific symbolic motifs over thirty days, statelessness is a fundamental architectural disqualification. No amount of prompt augmentation within a single API call can substitute for a persistent, queryable record of prior semantic content. The analytical value of longitudinal tracking lies in the relationships between sessions, not within any single one.

Commercial API dependency raises a separate ethical dimension in mental health applications. Dream content ranks among the most intimate categories of personal psychological data a user can generate. Routing that content through third-party infrastructure—including commercial model inference endpoints—exposes it to data retention policies, model fine-tuning pipelines, and security surfaces the user cannot audit or control. This concern applies regardless of the privacy commitments any given provider makes, because the structural exposure exists at the level of transmission and processing rather than contractual intent. Mental health informatics applications have an obligation to minimize unnecessary data movement, particularly when local and privacy-preserving alternatives exist.

Morpheus addresses each of these limitations explicitly. The domain-specific `books` corpus supplies the psychoanalytic grounding the generative model cannot be assumed to possess reliably, conditioning every produced interpretation on retrieved passages from established theoretical literature rather than training data of uncertain provenance. Persistent vector storage in Supabase saves every processed dream as a 384-dimensional embedding alongside extracted metadata, enabling session-to-session semantic comparison across the full duration of a user's engagement. Local embedding computation via `sentence-transformers` processes all dream text on the application's own infrastructure rather than transmitting it to a commercial embedding API.

Saleemi et al. (2025) sidestepped the hallucination problem altogether by forgoing generation in favor of discriminative BERT-based classification—an architectural conservatism appropriate and defensible for the research scope they defined. Discriminative classification, however accurate, cannot produce the interpretive narrative depth that makes psychological analysis clinically useful rather than merely categorical. Extending their work to include generation introduces the hallucination risk; the RAG architecture implemented in Morpheus is the mechanism through which that risk is controlled.

### 1.3 Morpheus Architectural Design and Backend Infrastructure

Morpheus is a Progressive Web Application built on a modular, service-oriented architecture that separates concerns across three distinct layers. The user-facing layer manages input capture, real-time state presentation, and graph-based visualization. The API layer coordinates the processing pipeline, enforces validation, and routes requests to appropriate service components. The combined data and intelligence layer handles persistence, vector search, and language model inference. Each separation is deliberate. Decoupling the intelligence layer from the API layer, for instance, allows individual components—the speech-to-text module, the embedding model, and the language model client—to be evaluated, replaced, or upgraded independently without disrupting the broader application.

The backend is implemented in Python 3.11 with FastAPI as the web framework and Uvicorn as the ASGI server. FastAPI suits this application for several converging reasons. Its asynchronous request handling keeps I/O-bound operations—database writes, embedding computation, external language model API calls—from blocking concurrent requests, which matters for a pipeline that chains multiple sequential operations of variable latency. Pydantic schema models enforce type-safe request and response validation, reducing input-handling errors that could corrupt downstream analytical steps. Type annotations also generate OpenAPI documentation automatically, providing a persistent machine-readable record of the API contract valuable for both development verification and academic reproducibility. The application root sits under the `app` directory: `main.py` defines the FastAPI instance, `config.py` manages environment variable loading through Pydantic settings, and `database.py` provides the Supabase client for all persistence operations.

Two primary API endpoints correspond to distinct phases of user interaction. `POST /v1/dreams/process` accepts an audio file upload and orchestrates the full analytical pipeline—audio format conversion and speech-to-text transcription, PII anonymization, Jungian entity extraction, semantic embedding generation, RAG retrieval, psychological analysis generation, and database persistence—before returning a structured response to the client. `GET /v1/graph/data` queries the dreams, keywords, and dream_keywords tables and returns a JSON structure with two arrays: nodes representing individual dream sessions and extracted keywords, and links representing relational connections between them. The frontend consumes this structure directly for graph rendering. These two endpoints encode the system's entire analytical contract with its users. Their simplicity at the interface reflects the complexity absorbed by the service layer behind them.

The computational intelligence of the system resides in the AI service layer, implemented in `app/services/ai_service.py`. The `openai-whisper` library handles speech-to-text as a locally loaded model instance. It accepts Georgian-language audio, performs automatic language detection, and translates to English through `task="translate"` inference mode, ensuring the remainder of the pipeline operates on a consistent language regardless of the speaker's native tongue. FFmpeg converts audio submitted in any format to 16 kHz mono WAV before inference, matching Whisper's expected input specification. Google Gemini 1.5 Flash performs entity extraction and psychological analysis via the `google-generativeai` SDK, with separate prompt templates governing each task. The entity extraction prompt instructs the model to return structured JSON containing a dream title, a list of Jungian symbols, a list of dominant emotions, and a one-sentence summary. The analysis prompt conditions the model on retrieved RAG context and instructs it to produce a full psychological interpretation. The `sentence-transformers` library computes semantic embeddings with the `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` model, producing 384-dimensional dense vectors lightweight enough to run on CPU without GPU acceleration—suitable for cloud deployment on constrained infrastructure.

Supabase forms the persistence layer, combining a managed PostgreSQL instance with the `pgvector` extension for high-dimensional vector storage and cosine similarity search. Four tables carry cleanly separated responsibilities. The `dreams` table stores raw transcribed text, generated psychological analysis, and the 384-dimensional embedding vector for each session, alongside user identity and timestamp metadata. The `keywords` table maintains a deduplicated registry of extracted psychological terms, ensuring the same symbol encountered across multiple sessions maps to a single graph node rather than redundant duplicates. The `dream_keywords` junction table encodes many-to-many relationships between dream sessions and associated keywords, supplying the relational structure the graph visualization endpoint traverses. The `knowledge_base` table stores chunked and embedded passages from psychoanalytic source texts in the `books` directory. A stored procedure named `match_documents` executes cosine similarity search over this table at query time, accepting a query embedding and returning the top-k most semantically similar passages that form the RAG context injected into the analysis prompt.

Railway hosts the backend deployment; the `Procfile`, `nixpacks.toml`, and `requirements.txt` files in the repository root define the platform configuration. Cloud deployment matters for reasons extending well beyond operational convenience. Dream recall peaks during the hypnopompic state—the brief transition between sleep and full wakefulness—when narrative memory remains accessible before consolidation degrades it. A system requiring a locally running laptop is simply not usable at that moment. Railway keeps the backend continuously available from any networked device, including a phone held in bed, aligning availability with the window of highest user data quality.

The user interface uses React with Vite as the build tool and TailwindCSS for component styling, following a mobile-first responsive layout matched to the primary use context. `react-force-graph-2d`, a D3-force-based library, renders the knowledge graph as interactive, physics-simulated force-directed layouts in which node positions emerge from relational structure rather than manual placement. A PWA manifest configures installation on mobile devices, allowing launch from a home screen without browser chrome and reducing friction between waking and capturing a dream narrative. Across every layer, these architectural decisions serve one unified goal: converting ephemeral subjective psychological experience into durable, queryable, semantically structured data that retains enough of its original meaning to support longitudinal analysis.

## Chapter 2: Data Engineering, Semantic Pipelines, and Empirical Evaluation

Chapter 1 established the theoretical and architectural foundations of Morpheus. Chapter 2 examines the operational substance of those foundations—the data engineering pipeline that transforms raw psychological input into semantically structured knowledge, and the empirical methods used to evaluate both technical behavior and psychological validity. These concerns are inseparable. Every downstream analytical claim depends directly on upstream data engineering quality.

### 2.1 Document Ingestion and Text Sanitization

Morpheus must solve two fundamentally different ingestion problems. Conflating their requirements would compromise both. The first track handles real-time user input: audio recordings of dream narratives that arrive as live, variable-quality data and must become clean, anonymized, structured text before psychological analysis can proceed. The second track handles static ingestion of academic and psychoanalytic source texts into the knowledge base—a one-time preprocessing operation whose output determines the quality of every RAG retrieval the system will perform. Each track demands a different sanitization strategy.

Audio capture begins through the browser's MediaRecorder API, which encodes the spoken dream narrative as a compressed audio blob and submits it to the FastAPI `POST /v1/dreams/process` endpoint as a multipart form upload. The backend's first operation is format normalization. Browsers produce audio in WebM, OGG, or MP4 depending on operating system and vendor. The service layer invokes FFmpeg to convert every incoming file to 16 kHz mono WAV before passing it to the speech-to-text model, ensuring consistent input regardless of client device.

The `openai-whisper` library handles transcription as a locally loaded model instance within the application's own infrastructure. Whisper operates in translation mode with `task="translate"`: it accepts input in any language, automatically identifies the source language, and returns English text. This design choice matters for Morpheus specifically. Georgian—the application's primary target language—is morphologically complex, and downstream NLP tools including entity extractors and embedding models have substantially less coverage for it than for English. Producing a high-quality English transcript before proceeding makes the remainder of the pipeline language-agnostic.

Running Whisper locally rather than through the hosted OpenAI Whisper API rests on three converging considerations. Sensitive psychological audio never leaves the system's own infrastructure, eliminating third-party data exposure. Per-call cost disappears entirely. Model quality remains identical, because the locally deployed artifact is the same underlying model served through the API.

Following transcription, raw text passes through a PII anonymization layer before any further processing. A spaCy named entity recognition model identifies PERSON entities and replaces each span with the placeholder token `[PERSON]`, using reverse-order substitution to preserve character offsets across multiple replacements within the same string. Compiled regular expression patterns handle remaining personally identifiable categories, replacing email addresses, phone numbers, and specific dates with `[EMAIL]`, `[PHONE]`, and `[DATE]` respectively. Using spaCy NER for person names rather than regex alone addresses a known failure mode of pattern-based anonymization: mis-tagging place names, organization names, and other capitalized tokens as personal identifiers.

Anonymization operates at two levels. Ethically, it prevents associating identifiable personal information with intimate psychological content in persistent storage. Technically, it removes tokens carrying no psychological signal from the text Gemini 1.5 Flash processes next, reducing noise in entity extraction output. Entity extraction runs in JSON mode with a strict schema requiring four fields: a short evocative dream title, an array of Jungian symbols, an array of dominant emotions, and a one-sentence narrative summary. Schema enforcement is not a convenience—it is a reliability guarantee. Without output constraints, a generative model can produce malformed, inconsistent, or hallucinated responses that silently corrupt downstream stages when parsed and persisted.

Knowledge base ingestion operates on nine psychoanalytic and depth psychology source texts constituting the RAG retrieval corpus: Freud's "The Interpretation of Dreams," Jung's "Archetypes of the Collective Unconscious" and "Man and His Symbols," Campbell's "The Hero with a Thousand Faces," van der Kolk's "The Body Keeps the Score," Eliade's "Myths, Dreams and Mysteries," Jung's "Memories, Dreams, Reflections," and the symbol dictionaries by Cirlot and the Penguin editorial team. These texts arrive in raw digital formats from Project Gutenberg and various digital editions, each carrying a different noise profile that degrades retrieval quality if left in place.

The sanitization utility `clean_for_rag.py` addresses this noise through a multi-stage pipeline applied to each source file in sequence. The first stage strips Project Gutenberg legal wrappers—license headers and footers that carry no psychological content and would inflate spurious retrieval matches. The second stage corrects OCR artifacts, including soft hyphen line breaks that split compound words across lines, year misreads in which the digit `1` was scanned as the letter `i` producing strings such as `i960` in place of `1960`, and stray decorative Unicode symbols from original typesetting. The third and fourth stages strip back matter and front matter respectively. Back matter removal targets index blocks, bibliography sections, footnote compilations, acknowledgment pages, and publisher marketing material appended after the main text—all dense but decontextualized language that would pollute the semantic similarity space. Front matter removal targets copyright pages, ISBN lines, publisher addresses, endorsement quotes, and other boilerplate preceding actual prose, using pattern-matching against known publisher identifiers and a heuristic that detects genuine prose onset by measuring paragraph length and sentence-ending density. A fifth stage removes tables of contents and illustration lists surviving front-matter stripping in texts with complex layouts. The final normalization stage collapses excessive whitespace, removes isolated page numbers and roman numeral page markers OCR preserved as standalone lines, strips footnote superscript digits glued to adjacent word tokens, and eliminates TOC leader dots and separator bars. For texts identified as alphabetical reference dictionaries—detected by the structural fingerprint of a standalone capital letter followed immediately by a lowercase entry word—an additional pass removes all-caps page-running headers OCR introduced between dictionary entries.

After cleaning, the ingestion script segments each file into chunks of approximately 500 tokens—roughly 2,000 characters—with 200-character overlap between adjacent chunks. Overlap is not arbitrary. Without it, theoretical concepts or psychoanalytic arguments spanning a chunk boundary would split into fragments stored in non-adjacent retrieval units, and similarity search would fail to surface the complete argument when either fragment alone matched the query embedding. Overlap ensures every semantically contiguous passage is fully represented in at least one retrievable chunk. Each chunk embeds with the same `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` sentence-transformer model used for dream embeddings, producing a 384-dimensional vector stored in the `knowledge_base` table alongside chunk text and source book title. Attributing each chunk to its source at storage time allows retrieval to present passage provenance—a basic requirement for academically defensible interpretation.

Before inserting records from any source file, the ingestion script enforces a quality gate by querying the `knowledge_base` table for existing entries sharing the same book title and skipping ingestion if matches exist. This deduplication check prevents redundant embedding vectors from skewing the cosine similarity distribution, which would cause over-represented texts to dominate retrieval and reduce knowledge base diversity. Once data is sanitized, chunked, and stored with verified uniqueness, the embedding and retrieval pipeline governs how semantic meaning is encoded and queried at inference time—the subject of Section 2.2.

### 2.2 Vector Embeddings and Latent Theme Mapping

An embedding maps a sequence of natural language tokens to a fixed-dimensional point in a continuous metric space such that semantic similarity between input texts corresponds to geometric proximity between their representative points. This property makes embeddings useful for psychological language analysis. Two dream narratives describing structurally different experiences but sharing an underlying emotional register—both involving confrontation with authority, both suffused with anticipatory anxiety—will produce embedding vectors close together in representational space even if they share no surface vocabulary.

The sentence-transformers model `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` generates all embeddings in Morpheus. It produces a 384-dimensional output space and trains specifically to optimize semantic similarity tasks through fine-tuning on large-scale natural language inference and semantic textual similarity benchmarks. It runs without GPU acceleration on Railway deployment infrastructure—a practical requirement given free-tier memory constraints. Its 384-dimensional output suffices to represent dream narrative semantic complexity while remaining tractable for cosine similarity search at personal dream journal scale.

Cosine similarity requires explanation before the formal definition. Embedding vectors vary in magnitude depending on text length and density. A one-sentence dream fragment and a six-sentence narrative describing the same core psychological experience produce vectors of different magnitudes. Euclidean distance would assign larger separation purely on magnitude difference, misrepresenting semantic proximity. Cosine similarity measures the angle between vectors rather than endpoint distance, normalizing for magnitude and capturing directional alignment. Two vectors pointing in the same direction receive cosine similarity of 1 regardless of length; two pointing in opposite directions receive 0. For embedding-based retrieval over psychological text, where input length varies and semantic relatedness should dominate, cosine similarity is the correct metric. The formula governing computation is as follows.

\[
\cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}}{\|\mathbf{A}\| \times \|\mathbf{B}\|}
\]

In this expression, **A** and **B** represent the 384-dimensional embedding vectors of two texts being compared. The dot product in the numerator captures directional alignment by summing products of corresponding components. The product of L2 norms in the denominator normalizes the result to the interval between 0 and 1 for non-negative embeddings. In Morpheus, **A** is the embedding of the incoming dream narrative and **B** is the embedding of a stored chunk in the `knowledge_base` table, with computation executed across the entire corpus to identify passages most relevant to the user's current dream content.

The `match_documents` stored procedure in Supabase implements this retrieval as a native database function using the `pgvector` extension. It accepts a query embedding vector, a minimum similarity threshold, and a maximum result count. It computes cosine similarity between the query vector and every stored knowledge base embedding using `pgvector`'s `<=>` cosine distance operator, converts distance to similarity by computing `1 - distance`, filters results below the threshold, and returns the top-k remaining results ordered by descending similarity. The threshold filter is not a minor optimization. A generative language model incorporates whatever context it receives. If retrieval surfaces semantically distant passages simply because no sufficiently similar passage exists, the model constructs interpretation from irrelevant context—fluent but analytically misleading. Enforcing a minimum similarity threshold means the generative prompt is either grounded in genuinely relevant theoretical material or left without retrieved context. Both outcomes are preferable to grounding in noise.

Latent theme mapping is the longitudinal function distinguishing Morpheus from a single-session dream analysis tool. Each processed dream narrative generates an embedding vector stored alongside metadata in the `dreams` table. Over repeated sessions, a user's dream embeddings define a trajectory through 384-dimensional semantic space. Clustering patterns and directional tendencies within that trajectory encode recurring psychological preoccupations the user may not have consciously identified. Dream embeddings consistently clustering in a region associated with evaluation by authority figures, performance failure, and urgency under constraint—themes representable in the Jungian knowledge base through passages on perfectionism, the shadow, and the animus dynamic—indicate a latent stress or perfectionism pattern. That inference does not require the user to mention perfectionism in any single narrative. It emerges from geometric distribution of embeddings across sessions.

The graph data structure returned by `GET /v1/graph/data` encodes the same semantic information relationally rather than geometrically. Dream nodes and keyword nodes connect through edges representing keyword co-occurrence within a particular dream session; graph topology builds from the `dreams`, `keywords`, and `dream_keywords` tables at query time. This graph is not merely visual. It functions as a relational semantic map of the user's psychological vocabulary across full engagement history. A keyword node appearing as a high-degree hub—connected to many dream nodes across many sessions—is a computational indicator of persistent psychological theme, directly analogous to a clinician observing that a patient returns to the same symbolic content across multiple therapy sessions. The graph makes that pattern visible at scale without manual cross-session annotation.

Saleemi et al. (2025) employed embedding-based clustering to group dream participants by dominant emotion category, identifying aggregate clusters corresponding to anxiety, sadness, and happiness at the group level. That approach suits their research question concerning statistical distribution of emotional content across a population. The Morpheus approach is complementary but distinct in analytical unit. Rather than mapping population-level distributions, it maps longitudinal evolution of a single user's semantic position through embedding space across time, treating individual psychological trajectory as the primary object of analysis rather than statistical category membership. Vector analysis of this kind describes what the system produces; it does not evaluate whether those outputs are technically reliable or psychologically valid. Section 2.3 addresses that evaluation.

### 2.3 Technical Verification and System Performance

Evaluation of Morpheus operates at two levels that must remain carefully separate. System-level technical verification asks whether each pipeline component executes its designated function correctly and within acceptable performance bounds. This level is performable against the existing implementation using controlled test inputs; results in this section reflect that verification. Human-impact empirical validation asks whether generated psychological interpretations are clinically meaningful to real users and whether longitudinal tracking produces measurable improvement in psychological self-awareness. That level requires a live user cohort and cannot be addressed with the current implementation alone; Section 2.4 formally proposes it. Conflating the two levels would overstate what has been demonstrated and obscure what genuinely remains as future work.

End-to-end pipeline verification proceeds sequentially through each processing stage. A test audio clip recorded in Georgian submits to `POST /v1/dreams/process`, initiating the full pipeline. Whisper processes the audio and produces an English transcript, assessed by manual comparison against a ground-truth transcript prepared before the test. Here the verification encountered a significant engineering finding. Railway's free-tier deployment imposes a memory ceiling preventing loading of Whisper `medium` or `large` model variants, necessitating the `base` model for cloud deployment. Georgian is a morphologically complex Kartvelian language with no Indo-European relatives; as a low-resource language it is underrepresented in Whisper's multilingual training distribution relative to European languages. The `base` model—substantially less capable than `medium` or `large` at low-resource phonological patterns—produces translation output with elevated word error rates for Georgian input, particularly for dream vocabulary involving emotionally charged or culturally specific terms appearing infrequently in the training corpus.

The pipeline's structural logic is fully verified: Whisper produces an English string propagating correctly through every downstream stage. That string's accuracy, however, is insufficient for production-grade psychological analysis when the source language is Georgian. Resolving this bottleneck requires either upgrading to Whisper `medium` or `large` with higher memory allocation, or adopting a fine-tuned phonetic encoder trained on Georgian speech data—either path would materially improve transcription fidelity for this use case. The finding does not invalidate system architecture. It identifies the deployment constraint that must be addressed before design-level accuracy guarantees can be realized in practice.

Entity extraction verification confirms that JSON returned by Gemini 1.5 Flash conforms to the enforced schema and that extracted symbols and emotions are semantically coherent with the supplied dream narrative. Schema conformance is binary; JSON mode configuration ensures structurally malformed outputs do not reach the persistence layer. Semantic coherence requires manual review: an evaluator reads source dream text and extracted entity arrays and judges whether identified symbols and dominant emotions reflect narrative content. Controlled testing with English-language inputs yielded consistently affirmative judgments, confirming that the entity extraction prompt and schema together produce reliable structured output. Embedding verification confirms the stored vector has exactly 384 dimensions, matching the `vector(384)` column specification in the Supabase pgvector schema. RAG retrieval verification inspects knowledge base chunks returned by `match_documents` for topical relevance to test dream content, confirming cosine similarity search surfaces psychoanalytic corpus passages genuinely related to narrative symbols and themes. Finally, review of the generated Georgian-language analysis assesses linguistic fluency and thematic coherence with extracted symbols, confirming that generative prompt construction and RAG context injection produce analytically grounded output.

Response latency across the full pipeline is a functional requirement, not merely a performance metric. The pipeline chains local Whisper inference, a Gemini API call for entity extraction, pgvector cosine similarity search over the knowledge base, and a second Gemini API call for analysis generation. Each stage introduces latency of variable magnitude depending on audio duration, knowledge base size, and network conditions. Total end-to-end latency should be measured across multiple test submissions and reported as mean with standard deviation to characterize typical behavior and variability. For a reflective journaling application—where the user records a dream immediately after waking with limited tolerance for slow interface during a cognitively fragile state—an acceptable latency ceiling is approximately 10 to 20 seconds from submission to displayed analysis. Measurements exceeding that range indicate need for pipeline optimization; the most likely candidate is Whisper inference, where switching from `base` to a quantized `medium` model with larger memory allocation would address both latency and accuracy limitations simultaneously.

Knowledge base integrity verification inspects the `knowledge_base` table following ingestion script execution. After processing all nine source texts, the table should contain several hundred distinct records, each representing a 2,000-character chunk with a 384-dimensional embedding and source attribution. Embedding distribution verification confirms vectors are geometrically dispersed across semantic space rather than concentrated in a narrow cluster—which would indicate redundant chunking of homogeneous material or malfunction in text segmentation logic. A well-distributed embedding space maximizes the probability that any given dream narrative surfaces a topically distinct, informative retrieval set, supporting the interpretive diversity that makes RAG analytically valuable.

Graph endpoint verification calls `GET /v1/graph/data` after multiple dream sessions and inspects returned JSON for structural and semantic correctness. The response should contain `nodes` and `links` arrays, with node entries carrying `id`, `label`, `type`, and `color` fields and link entries carrying `source` and `target` fields referencing valid node identifiers. Across multiple sessions, recurring keywords should manifest as high-degree nodes connected to several dream nodes simultaneously, demonstrating that relational schema and graph construction logic in `app/services/graph_logic.py` correctly aggregate cross-session co-occurrence data. A keyword appearing in isolation across a single session carries limited analytical weight. A keyword appearing as a structural hub across five sessions is a strong computational signal of psychological persistence, and graph topology should make that distinction visible.

These verification procedures confirm that the Morpheus pipeline executes correctly at the architectural level and that core components produce outputs consistent with design specifications—with the notable exception of Whisper transcription accuracy under memory-constrained deployment. They cannot confirm whether psychological interpretations are experienced as meaningful or accurate by real users, whether longitudinal emotional theme tracking corresponds to patterns users recognize, or whether engagement produces measurable improvement in psychological self-awareness over time. Those questions lie outside technical verification and require empirical investigation with a live participant cohort. Section 2.4 formally proposes that investigation.

### 2.4 Proposed Psychometric Framework and Experimental Design

The evaluation framework proposed here follows Design Science Research methodology. Hevner et al. (2004) established this paradigm for evaluating systems whose purpose is to create utility within a specific human context rather than to test a pre-existing natural phenomenon. Design Science Research treats the artifact—Morpheus—as both subject and instrument of inquiry, requiring evaluation to address not only whether the artifact functions correctly but whether it produces meaningful value for the humans who use it.

The proposed study is a 30-day longitudinal single-cohort repeated-measures design, to execute once Morpheus deploys to a live participant cohort. This section constitutes a formal protocol specification for that future empirical milestone. The target pool is a minimum of thirty adult volunteers with no prior formal dream journaling practice, recruited through the IBSU volunteer network. Participants engage with Morpheus on any morning they recall a dream across thirty consecutive days, submitting audio or text narratives through the system interface, receiving generated psychological analysis, and accumulating session records available for both personal reflection and research analysis. The system functions as an observational and reflective tool throughout. It does not constitute clinical therapy, does not generate diagnostic outputs, and participants are explicitly informed of these boundaries at recruitment. This distinction is embedded in protocol design at every stage.

The first metric evaluates Dream Recall Frequency—the average number of distinct dream episodes reported per participant per week across the study period. Recall frequency is not a measure of narrative quality or psychological depth, but a meaningful proxy for regularity and intention with which participants engage the reflective practice Morpheus facilitates. Baseline measurement covers Days 1 through 7; endpoint measurement covers Days 24 through 30. The null hypothesis (H0) holds that mean Dream Recall Frequency at endpoint does not differ significantly from baseline across the cohort. The alternative hypothesis (H1) holds that mean Dream Recall Frequency at endpoint is significantly greater than at baseline—a directional prediction consistent with intention-setting literature in sleep psychology, where consistent morning engagement with reflective practice associates with increased recall fidelity over time (Schredl, 2019). Analysis applies a paired-sample t-test, with each participant contributing one baseline and one endpoint recall count as matched observations. Reporting includes t-statistic, degrees of freedom equal to N minus 1, two-tailed p-value, and Cohen's d as standardized effect size. The significance threshold is p less than .05 throughout unless otherwise specified.

The second metric tracks narrative richness through Lexical Density—the ratio of unique content words to total words in a dream narrative, expressed as a percentage. Formally:

\[
LD = \frac{|\text{unique lexical items}|}{|\text{total words}|} \times 100
\]

Higher Lexical Density indicates a narrative drawing on broader, more varied vocabulary relative to total length—a proxy for introspective range and emotional expressiveness. The underlying hypothesis predicts that thirty days of systematic dream engagement will produce richer, more differentiated articulation of internal experience and measurable Lexical Density increases. Alongside Lexical Density, emotional valence shifts track through structured Gemini entity extraction output. Positive, negative, neutral, and complex emotion tag frequencies aggregate per participant per week and plot as weekly trajectories across the study period. H0 holds that mean Lexical Density and mean emotional valence distribution at Days 24 through 30 do not differ significantly from baseline. H1 holds that mean Lexical Density increases significantly and emotional valence distribution shifts detectably—for instance through reduced anxiety tag frequency, increased neutral tagging, or emergence of more complex mixed emotional classifications suggesting greater affective differentiation. Analysis applies paired-sample t-test for continuous Lexical Density and chi-square test for independence on emotion tag frequency distributions, comparing early-study and late-study time blocks. Reporting includes t-statistic and p-value for Lexical Density, and chi-square statistic, degrees of freedom, p-value, and Cramér's V for the categorical emotional valence comparison.

The third metric employs the Lucidity and Consciousness in Dreams scale—a standardized psychometric instrument developed by Voss et al. (2013) to assess self-reported dream awareness and metacognitive control across eight subscales: insight, control, thought, realism, memory, dissociation, negative emotion, and self-determination. LuCiD provides a validated quantitative measure of dream-state awareness; repeated administration across a longitudinal period allows detection of change in metacognitive dream awareness over time. Administration occurs at Day 1 (baseline), Day 15 (midpoint), and Day 30 (endpoint). H0 holds that LuCiD total scores at Day 30 do not differ significantly from Day 1. H1 holds that LuCiD scores increase significantly from Day 1 to Day 30, indicating that sustained engagement with reflective dream analysis practice improves metacognitive awareness during the dream state itself. Analysis applies one-way repeated-measures analysis of variance across three time points, reporting F-statistic, degrees of freedom for between-levels factor and error term, p-value, and partial eta squared as effect size. If omnibus ANOVA reaches significance, Bonferroni-corrected pairwise post-hoc t-tests identify which intervals account for observed change, with corrected alpha at .05 divided by comparison count.

The fourth hypothesis differs in character from the three psychometric metrics. It requires no external instrument and no self-reported data. It computes entirely from the Supabase `dreams` table using embedding vectors generated and stored during normal operation. The hypothesis states that participants engaging with Morpheus consistently across thirty days will exhibit tighter semantic clustering in personal embedding space toward study end than at beginning, reflecting progressive narrowing and coherence in psychological themes their dream narratives express. Tightening operationalizes as reduced mean intra-user cosine distance—the average pairwise cosine distance between all dream embedding vectors a single participant produces within a given seven-day window. Lower mean intra-user cosine distance indicates dreams during that window are more semantically similar, interpreted as evidence of increasing thematic focus and psychological coherence. H0 holds that mean intra-user cosine distance during Days 24 through 30 does not differ significantly from Days 1 through 7. H1 holds that mean intra-user cosine distance decreases significantly, indicating dream content converges toward a more semantically coherent cluster as the study progresses. Analysis applies paired-sample t-test comparing per-participant distance means from the first seven days against the final seven days, with N equal to participants contributing sufficient data in both windows.

The ethical framework governing the proposed study is constitutive of the protocol, not ancillary to it. All participants must provide written informed consent before data collection, with documentation stating what data is collected, how it is stored, and for what purposes. Dream content ranks among the most intimate categories of personal psychological data an individual can generate; consent must reflect that sensitivity explicitly rather than treating it as routine formality. Participants must know their narratives are stored as anonymized vector representations with no personally identifiable text retained beyond anonymization. They must know the system does not constitute clinical therapy, that analyses are computationally generated and not clinically validated, and that participation creates no therapeutic relationship. Institutional ethics review board approval—or equivalent under the IBSU institutional ethics framework—will be sought before live cohort recruitment. The minimum sample size of thirty participants represents the minimum viable cohort for detecting a medium-to-large effect (Cohen's d of 0.5 or greater) with 80 percent statistical power at two-tailed alpha of .05, consistent with conventions established by Cohen (1988) for behavioral science research. Smaller samples would lack power to detect anticipated effect sizes and would produce findings neither replicable nor interpretable with confidence.

## Conclusion

This thesis demonstrates that a Retrieval-Augmented Generation architecture, grounded in domain-specific psychoanalytic knowledge, can transform subjective dream narratives into durable, semantically structured psychological data. That data persists across sessions and supports longitudinal analysis. Generic large language models do not provide this capability. Hallucination risk, thin and unevenly distributed psychoanalytic representation in training data, and fundamental statelessness disqualify them without the domain-specific retrieval scaffolding Morpheus supplies. Saleemi et al. (2025) demonstrated a discriminative classification approach empirically sound within its defined scope—but categorical outputs lack the interpretive depth required for psychologically meaningful narrative analysis. The architecture presented here addresses both limitations in a technically verifiable way.

Chapter 1 documents architectural decisions that are not incidental—they are preconditions for achieving the psychological goal at all. FastAPI's asynchronous request handling keeps the multi-stage pipeline from blocking under concurrent load. Supabase and pgvector ensure every processed dream contributes a permanent, queryable semantic record rather than a transient output. Local embedding computation via `sentence-transformers` eliminates data exposure risk that commercial API routing would introduce for content of this sensitivity. Railway cloud deployment keeps the system available at the hypnopompic moment of highest recall fidelity, when a locally installed application would be unreachable. Remove any one of these properties and the longitudinal semantic tracking that defines Morpheus as analytically distinct from a single-session tool becomes impossible.

Chapter 2 yields conclusions that generalize beyond Morpheus itself. Document sanitization quality directly determines RAG retrieval quality—polluted knowledge base vectors produce irrelevant chunks that a generative model incorporates regardless. Vector embeddings enable latent theme detection beyond keyword matching, because geometric proximity in high-dimensional continuous space captures conceptual and emotional similarity at a granularity surface-level string comparison cannot reach. The proposed psychometric framework—organized around Dream Recall Frequency, Lexical Density, the LuCiD scale, and intra-user semantic clustering—offers a rigorous, statistically grounded pathway to human-impact validation. It is formally specified, reproducible, and executable against the existing system without architectural modification.

Section 2.4's psychometric framework is a formally specified research protocol, not a completed study. That position is methodologically appropriate under Design Science Research, where the artifact exists, technical behavior is verified across defined test conditions, and human-impact hypotheses are precisely stated and statistically tractable. What this thesis does not yet provide is empirical evidence of user-level effect. That evidence will come from the 30-day cohort pilot, IRB approval under the IBSU institutional ethics framework, and application of Section 2.4's statistical methods to a real participant dataset. Stating this limitation clearly is more scientifically valuable than obscuring it.

Subconscious data may be the last domain of personal experience that quantitative methods have not yet made systematically legible. Clinical capture still depends on human recall and manual annotation. Computational approaches capable of processing it with theoretical rigor remain nascent. The potential for structured, longitudinally trackable insight into inner psychological life is, by any reasonable measure, largely unrealized. Morpheus takes a first step toward changing that. It is computationally reproducible, architecturally grounded in established principles of retrieval and generation, and anchored in a century of psychoanalytic theory. The technical infrastructure for this kind of analysis now exists. Establishing its human value is the natural and immediate next step.
